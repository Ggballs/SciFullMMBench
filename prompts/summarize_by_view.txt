# Literature-Review Style Summarization

You are given the full OpenReview content for a scientific paper:
- Reviewer summaries, strengths, weaknesses, and questions (all reviewers)
- Author rebuttals
- Area chair meta review

Your task: produce structured summaries for each view in a **literature review style**.

Write as if you are preparing high-quality structured notes for a related-work section or paper survey.
The output should describe the paper's contribution, method, evidence, and limits in clean academic prose,
not narrate the review conversation.

Each view should contain:
- a short view-specific summary
- a consolidated list of bullet points capturing the distinct and important points for that view

## Rules:
1. One atomic claim per bullet
2. Merge duplicate points across reviewers into one bullet (keep the most specific version)
3. Prioritize the multimodal information (exactly pointing out the table / figure / equation...)
4. Exclude procedural comments (e.g. "thanks for the review")
5. Categorize each point into exactly one best-fit view:
   - contribution
   - method/dataset
   - experiment/result
   - limitation/scope
6. Do not repeat or paraphrase the same point across views or within a view
7. If a point could fit multiple views, assign it once to the most specific view
8. The `summary` of a view must only summarize the bullet points in that same view
9. Write every `summary` and bullet point in a **literature review tone**: concise, academic, descriptive, and paper-focused
11. Describe the paper itself, not the conversation around it
12. Do not write bullets like "Reviewer 2 asks...", "A reviewer questions...", "The rebuttal explains...", or "The authors respond that..."
14. When a reviewer concern is clarified or resolved by the rebuttal or comments, absorb that clarification into the final statement rather than narrating the exchange
15. When a concern remains only partially resolved, express that as a paper-level limitation or evidential weakness
16. Prefer formulations such as:
    - "The work introduces..."
    - "The method uses..."
    - "The dataset is constructed by..."
    - "Experiments show..."
    - "Evidence remains limited for..."
17. If a claim is disputed or only weakly supported, use calibrated wording such as:
    - "The paper argues..."
    - "The reported evidence suggests..."
    - "Support for X remains limited to..."
18. Focus on the final interpreted substance of the paper and its evidence, not the conversational back-and-forth

## Output Format:
Output a JSON object with the following structure:
```
{{
  "contribution": {{
    "summary": "short summary for the contribution view",
    "bullet_points": ["bullet point 1", "bullet point 2", ...]
  }},
  "method_dataset": {{
    "summary": "short summary for the method/dataset view",
    "bullet_points": ["bullet point 1", "bullet point 2", ...]
  }},
  "experiment_result": {{
    "summary": "short summary for the experiment/result view",
    "bullet_points": ["bullet point 1", "bullet point 2", ...]
  }},
  "limitation_scope": {{
    "summary": "short summary for the limitation/scope view",
    "bullet_points": ["bullet point 1", "bullet point 2", ...]
  }}
}}
```


## Important Interpretation Rule:
- The OpenReview discussion is evidence, not the output style.
- Use it to decide what is well supported, what is clarified, and what remains uncertain.
- Then rewrite everything as clean literature-review statements about the paper.
